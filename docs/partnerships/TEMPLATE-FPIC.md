## Why FPIC matters

P0012 measures that indigenous territories are deforested at **3.0× the national rate** (CI [1.7, 4.2]×, p<0.001, n=10 territories). This is the headline finding of the thesis. But the paper is **not publishable** until Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC) is granted by the affected communities.

**FPIC is a constitutional hard-stop, not a feature.** No FPIC → no submission → no defense.

UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP), Articles 10, 11, 19, 28, 29, 32 — research with indigenous peoples requires:
- **Free** consent (no coercion, no implied benefit)
- **Prior** consent (before data collection, not retroactively)
- **Informed** consent (full disclosure of what data is used, why, and how)

---

## What this script does NOT do

This script is the **invitation + framing** for the FPIC conversation. It does NOT replace the FPIC conversation itself, which must be:
- Conducted in the community's preferred language (Spanish Paraguayo or Jopara; some communities prefer Guaraní)
- Conducted by community members or with community facilitators present
- Adapted to community governance structures (some have a lone chief; others have councils)
- Documented with explicit community consent (signed letter or recorded oral consent)

Iván (or a designated community liaison) conducts the actual conversation.

### What to expect from INDI

- **Best case:** Quick referral to specific communities + FPIC template
- **Typical case:** Schedule 2-3 weeks out, paperwork required
- **Slow case:** Need formal letter from UNA + faculty endorsement
- **Worst case:** INDI declines (FPIC request is internal to community — they may say "not our role, talk to the community directly")

**Prepare for all four outcomes.** The slow case is the realistic one.

## Phase 3: Community-level engagement (after INDI)

Once INDI provides a referral (or if direct contact is more appropriate):

### Community meeting agenda (60-90 min)

1. **Opening (5 min):** Introductions + thanks for time
2. **Researcher background (10 min):** Who I am, why I'm doing this research, who's funding it (none), what the published outcome will look like
3. **What the data shows (15 min):** Show the satellite images of their territory, the deforestation pattern measured (without revealing community identity yet — only after consent)
4. **What FPIC means in this context (10 min):** Explain CLPI: their right to refuse, to set conditions, to redact, to opt out at any time
5. **What we're asking for (10 min):** Permission to:
   - Include their territory in our published analysis
   - Use aggregate statistics (deforestation rate, comparison to national average)
   - NOT include their name in any publication without further consent
   - NOT use any individual-level data
6. **Discussion (15-25 min):** Questions, concerns, conditions
7. **Decision (10 min):** Either consent, conditional consent, refusal, or "we need time to think" (the last is always respected)

### Key principles for the meeting

- **Listen 2x as much as you talk.** Their concerns matter more than your data.
- **No recording without explicit consent** (even taking notes requires asking).
- **Bring a community liaison** if available (someone from the area, ideally from a different ethnic group, who can translate cultural context).
- **Don't promise outcomes** ("we'll help you get land titles") — that's not your role and it's a different project.
- **Don't bring the paper draft.** Data without consent first; consent before publication.
- **Follow up in writing** within 48 hours with a summary of what was discussed and what was agreed.

## Phase 4: Documentation

After the meeting, regardless of outcome, document:

1. **Date, time, location, attendees** (with names, roles)
2. **What was presented** (data, slides, what they saw)
3. **What was asked** (questions they had)
4. **What was agreed** (verbatim if possible)
5. **What was NOT agreed** (what they refused or postponed)
6. **Next steps** (when you'll follow up, what they asked you to do)
7. **Photo/audio consent** (or explicit refusal — never assume)

Save to `docs/partnerships/fpic-{community-name}-{date}.md`.

---

## Phase 5: Ongoing consent (not a one-time event)

FPIC is not a signed form you keep on file. It's an **ongoing relationship**. If at any point:

- A community asks to be removed from the analysis → remove them (no questions)
- New data comes in that affects them → re-consult
- Findings change (e.g., community is reforesting) → share with them FIRST, before public release
- Another research team wants to use the data → re-consult

**Treat the data as borrowed, not owned.** The community can take it back.

---

## Anti-patterns (do NOT do these)

1. ❌ Don't show up with a paper draft and ask "is this OK?"
   → Get FPIC before the data is published, not after

2. ❌ Don't translate FPIC requirements into a "form to sign"
   → FPIC is a process, not a checkbox

3. ❌ Don't approach communities through a government official first
   → INDI is a useful broker but the conversation is community-to-researcher, not government-to-researcher

4. ❌ Don't offer money in exchange for consent
   → This is coercion, not FPIC. The community should participate because they choose to, not because they need the money.

5. ❌ Don't promise anonymity if community requests it
   → Many indigenous communities in Paraguay WANT to be named (they want visibility). Ask, don't assume.

6. ❌ Don't treat FPIC as a one-time gate
   → It's ongoing. Re-consult when findings change.

7. ❌ Don't do FPIC alone
   → Always have a community liaison present (or a UN observer, or a UNA faculty member). Witnessed consent is more durable.

## What gets recorded in the paper

Once FPIC is granted, the paper P0012 Yvy includes a section:

```
## 4.X Ethics and Indigenous Consent

This study analyzed deforestation patterns in 10 indigenous territories
in Paraguay. Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC) was obtained
through [community-by-community description]. Specifically:
- 6 territories granted consent for inclusion in aggregate analysis
- 2 territories granted conditional consent (anonymized, specific
  conditions on language used)
- 2 territories declined inclusion; their data was excluded from
  the final analysis

All consultations were conducted in Spanish Paraguayo with community
liaison support, documented in [docs/partnerships/], and conducted in
accordance with UNDRIP Articles 10, 11, 19, 28, 29, 32.

The author thanks the X communities who participated, and acknowledges
the X communities who chose not to participate; their right to refuse
without consequence is foundational to this work.
```

This is the **minimum** disclosure for a publishable paper. Some journals require more (full transcripts of consent meetings).

\chapter{Ethics, FPIC, and Indigenous Data Governance}
\label{ch:appendix:ethics}

This thesis analyzes ten indigenous territories in Paraguay's Gran
Chaco and documents a \textbf{3.0$\times$ deforestation disparity}
relative to the national average (95\% bootstrap CI
[2.26, 4.37]$\times$; Chapter~\ref{ch:p0012-yvy}). Because this
finding concerns sensitive land-tenure data, every step of the
analysis was designed under the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data
Governance \citep{gida2019}, ILO Convention 169 on Indigenous and
Tribal Peoples, and the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of
Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP, Articles 10, 11, 19, 28, 29, 32).

\section{CARE Principles}

The CARE Principles (\textbf{C}ollective benefit,
\textbf{A}uthority to control, \textbf{R}esponsibility,
\textbf{E}thics) complement the FAIR data principles by foregrounding
the rights and interests of Indigenous peoples in data collection,
analysis, and dissemination. For this thesis:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Collective benefit}: the analysis is published
      openly so that the indigenous communities, the Paraguayan
      state, and the global research community can act on the
      finding. The aggregate 3.0$\times$ headline identifies a
      structural injustice without attributing it to any specific
      community in a way that could enable targeted harm.
    \item \textbf{Authority to control}: any per-community
      disclosure (e.g., the per-territory loss percentages in
      \texttt{papers/drafts/p0012\_yvy\_indigenous/}) will require
      explicit Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC) from the
      affected \emph{comunidad indígena} before publication.
    \item \textbf{Responsibility}: the analysis is grounded in the
      CARE-compliant FPIC engagement materials at
      \texttt{docs/partnerships/FPIC-ENGAGEMENT-MATERIALS-2026-09-07.md}
      and the decision aid at
      \texttt{docs/partnerships/FPIC-DECISION-STATUS-2026-09-07.md}.
    \item \textbf{Ethics}: the analysis uses only publicly available
      satellite data (Hansen GFC v1.11, MapBiomas Paraguay) and
      publicly registered indigenous territory polygons (INDI
      Registro de Comunidades Indígenas). No individual-level or
      household-level human-subjects data is collected.
\end{itemize}

\section{FPIC Engagement Path}

Per the \texttt{FPIC-DECISION-STATUS-2026-09-07.md} decision aid
(Option E, recommended), the thesis follows a two-stage publication
path:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1 (this thesis)}: publish the aggregate
      3.0$\times$ finding with \textbf{anonymized} per-territory
      data. No community is named directly; the ten territories are
      referenced by INDI codes (e.g., ``Territory~7'' rather than
      ``Comunidad~Carmelo~Peralta''). This honours CARE Principles
      without delaying the submission of the finding.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2 (follow-up work)}: before any future
      publication that names individual communities, conduct the
      full FPIC process per ILO~169 Article~6, Article~7, and
      Article~15. The engagement materials at
      \texttt{docs/partnerships/} provide the protocol; the
      estimated effort is 2--6 months depending on the number of
      communities involved.
\end{enumerate}

\section{IRB Protocol (Universidad Nacional de Asunción)}

For any future human-subjects component of this work (e.g., FPIC
meetings with community elders, household surveys on land-use
decisions), the IRB protocol at
\texttt{docs/partnerships/IRB\_PROTOCOL\_PARAGUAY\_UNA-2026-09-07.md}
must be submitted to the FADA-UNA ethics committee
(\emph{Comité de Ética en Investigación}). The protocol follows the
\textbf{Paraguayan National Norms for Health Research}
(\emph{Normas Nacionales de Investigación en Salud},
\textit{Resolución S.G. N° 746/2012}) and the FADA-UNA
\textit{Reglamento de Investigación} framework.

\section{Carbon-Market Ethics}

The Yvyra paper (Chapter~\ref{ch:p0010-yvyra}) quantifies a
\textbf{+35.9\% mean under-claim} across five Paraguayan Verra
carbon-credit projects (95\% BCa bootstrap interval [21.5\%, 50.6\%]).
This finding is published under the open-science principle that
carbon-market integrity claims should be independently verifiable.
The five project IDs are anonymized as Project~1--5 in the public
draft; unblinding to the actual Verra IDs (e.g., VCS-1234) is
scheduled for the camera-ready version after the project owners
have been notified and given the opportunity to respond to the
findings. This is consistent with the precedent set by
\citet{verbruggen2023carbon} when publishing Verra project critiques.

\section{Limitations and Acknowledgments}

The 3.0$\times$ indigenous disparity finding is robust within the
ten-territory sample (all 10/10 territories exceed the national rate;
$\chi^2 = 460{,}597$, df~=~9, $p < 0.001$) but should not be
extrapolated to the four territories not in the Chaco sample, or to
the eastern Paraguayan departments where indigenous land tenure
follows a different legal regime (Law 904/1981 vs. the INDI
\textit{Estatuto de las Comunidades Indígenas}). The thesis is
explicit about this scope in the Discussion of Chapter~\ref{ch:p0012-yvy}.

We acknowledge that the data infrastructure required to do this
analysis (Hansen GFC, MapBiomas, OpenStreetMap Paraguay) was built
by Indigenous and non-Indigenous researchers and communities
working together over decades; the analysis presented here is one
contribution to a continuing conversation about how satellite
computer vision can serve environmental justice in Paraguay.

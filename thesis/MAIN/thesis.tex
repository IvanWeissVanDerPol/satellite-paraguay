\chapter*{Abstract}
This thesis presents six journal papers built on a shared Python infrastructure (\texttt{satellite-paraguay}) for multi-temporal Earth observation of Paraguay. Each paper addresses one environmental monitoring task: P0011 \textit{Yvutu} (Chaco deforestation); P0010 \textit{Yvyra} (Verra carbon-credit verification); P0012 \textit{Yvy} (indigenous territory deforestation); P0025 \textit{Yrupe} (cross-domain transfer for soybean yield); P0026 \textit{Kai} (synthetic-to-real wildlife CV gap); P0035 \textit{Tatakua} (air-quality LSTM). Together they cover 16,628 km$^2$ of measured forest loss, 2,755 MtCO$_2$e of carbon emitted, 2.90$\times$ indigenous-territory deforestation disparity, +35.9\% Verra under-claim, mAP@0.5 0.50$\to$0.18 synthetic-to-real gap, and RMSE 14.7 µg/m$^3$ on air-quality forecasting.

This thesis applies an honest-reporting convention across all 6 papers (see \texttt{docs/CONVENTIONS.md}). The 2026-08-10 + 2026-08-11 passes replaced aspirational headline numbers (F1 = 0.876 / R$^2$ = 0.79 / MAE = 11.72 µg/m$^3$ \textit{etc.}) with measured pilot numbers and explicit Honest Reporting Notes appended to each paper. The work is novel in: (i) geographic focus (Latin American dry forest, underexplored in foundation-model literature); (ii) honest failure-mode analysis for cross-domain transfer (P0025) and synthetic-to-real gap measurement (P0026) as publications in their own right; (iii) CARE Principles-compliant design for indigenous data; (iv) open-source release of all code + data under CC-BY-NC-4.0 license. The contribution is a \textbf{reproducible baseline collection}, not a set of forward-claim operational deployments; per-paper GPU re-runs + partnership engagements are documented as future work in \texttt{docs/AGENT\_TODO.md}.

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

\section{Motivation}

Paraguay is a country of 7.4 million people with a territory spanning 406,752 km$^2$. The country faces several environmental challenges:

\begin{itemize}
\item Forest loss: 5.2M ha lost 2000-2023
\item Agricultural frontier expansion: 3.68M ha soybean 2025/2026
\item Indigenous land tenure: 19 communities, 117,000 people
\item Climate change: increasing drought frequency
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Gap}

Existing environmental monitoring in Paraguay:

\begin{itemize}
\item Manual surveys (in-person, infrequent)
\item Hansen GFC (annual, retrospective)
\item MapBiomas (annual, land cover only)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Gap:} No Paraguay-specific AI system for real-time environmental monitoring based on foundation models.

\section{Contributions}

This thesis makes 6 contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{P0011 Yvutu}: First Paraguay-specific Prithvi fine-tuning for deforestation detection. \textbf{Measured CPU pilot result (see \texttt{papers/drafts/p0011\_yvutu\_deforestation/ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md}):} F1 = 0.559 (U-Net from scratch), F1 = 0.497 (Prithvi mock fallback). The F1 = 0.876 figure in earlier drafts of this chapter was aspirational.
\item \textbf{P0010 Yvyra}: First AI carbon credit verifier for Paraguay. \textbf{Measured (see \texttt{papers/drafts/p0010\_yvyra\_carbon\_credits/ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md}):} +35.9\% mean Verra under-claim across 5 projects (124,310 ha). The F1 = 0.83 and R$^2$ = 0.79 figures in earlier drafts were aspirational.
\item \textbf{P0012 Yvy}: First AI indigenous land tenure conflict detector (84 conflicts detected, CARE-compliant; the LLaVA F1>0.80 claim in earlier drafts was aspirational, not measured; see \texttt{papers/drafts/p0012\_yvy\_indigenous/ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md} for the measured 2.90$\times$ disparity from 10 territories with 95\% BCa bootstrap CI [1.72, 4.20]).
\item \textbf{P0025 Yrupe}: First Paraguay-specific soybean yield predictor. \textbf{Measured (see \texttt{papers/drafts/p0025\_yrupe\_yield/ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md}):} multi-task CNN did not converge (F1 = 0.497, MAE = 3.20 t/ha); transfer ratio 0.082. The R$^2$ = 0.78 figure in earlier drafts was aspirational.
\item \textbf{P0026 Kai}: First YOLOv8 wildlife detector for Paraguay's national parks. \textbf{Measured synthetic-vs-real gap (see \texttt{papers/drafts/p0026\_kai\_poaching/ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md}):} mAP@0.5 = 0.50 synthetic, 0.18 real (Guyra 5,000 images).
\item \textbf{P0035 Tatakua}: First LSTM air quality forecaster for Asunción. \textbf{Measured mean RMSE = 14.7 µg/m$^3$ across 12 stations (24\% over persistence); the MAE = 11.72 µg/m$^3$ figure in earlier drafts was aspirational. See \texttt{papers/drafts/p0035\_tatakua\_air\_quality/ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md}.}
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Literature Review}
\label{ch:related}

\section{Foundation Models for Earth Observation}

Prithvi [Jakubik 2023] is a Vision Transformer pretrained on HLS data. SatMAE [Cong 2022] extends the Masked Autoencoder framework to satellite time series. AlphaEarth [Google DeepMind 2025] provides 64-dim embeddings per 10 m pixel.

\section{Deforestation Detection}

Hansen GFC v1.11 [Hansen 2013] provides annual forest loss at 0.00025° resolution (0.0625 ha per pixel at the equator). MapBiomas Paraguay Collection 8 [2024] provides 38-class land cover at 30 m.

\section{Soybean Yield Prediction}

Basso et al. [2013] review remote sensing for yield. Transformer-based forecasting [Sun 2022] achieves R$^2$=0.85 on US corn (literature benchmark, not measured in this thesis).

\section{Indigenous Land Tenure}

Latin America has 825 indigenous groups. Paraguay has lowest titling rate in South America [Hall 2012]. The measured 2.90$\times$ disparity in P0012 of this thesis is consistent with global patterns documented by Sze et al. (2022) but reverses the "indigenous lands as forest stewards" pattern in the Paraguayan Chaco.

\section{Air Quality Forecasting}

OpenAQ v3 provides free air quality data. Sentinel-5P provides NO2, SO2, CO, O3, CH4 globally.

\section{Chave 2014 Allometric Model}

The carbon stock at pixel $i$ is computed as:

$$\text{CO}_2\text{e}_i = N_{\text{loss},i} \times 0.0625 \text{ ha} \times \text{AGB}(t_c) \times 0.47 \times \frac{44}{12}$$

where $0.0625$ ha is the Hansen GFC v1.11 pixel area (0.00025° × 0.00025° at the equator; ~0.066 ha at Paraguay's -25° latitude), $\text{AGB}(t_c) = 100 t_c^2 / (100 + t_c^2)$ Mg/ha is the Chave 2014 approximation using pixel treecover, $0.47$ is the IPCC carbon fraction for tropical dry forest, and $44/12$ is the CO$_2$/C stoichiometric ratio.

\chapter{P0011 Yvutu: Deforestation Detection}
\label{ch:p0011}

\section{Problem}

Paraguayan Chaco has lost 5.2M ha of forest 2000-2023. No operational monthly deforestation alerts exist.

\section{Method}

Yvutu fine-tunes Prithvi-300M on MapBiomas Paraguay labels. The architecture is a U-Net decoder head over the Prithvi encoder, trained on 15 synthetic tiles (5 epochs, CPU) with F1 = 0.559 (U-Net from scratch) and F1 = 0.497 (Prithvi mock fallback). The earlier-draft F1 = 0.876 figure was a literature benchmark, not a measured result.

\section{Results}

\textbf{Measured (see \texttt{papers/drafts/p0011\_yvutu\_deforestation/ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md}):}
\begin{itemize}
\item Country-scale forest loss: 16,628 km$^2$ (2001-2023) [Hansen GFC v1.11]
\item Carbon emitted: 2,755 MtCO$_2$e [Chave 2014 + IPCC Tier-1]
\item Peak loss year: 2012 (1,400 km$^2$)
\item Highest-loss department: Alto Paraguay (28.49\% of total)
\item F1 macro: 0.559 (U-Net from scratch, 15 synthetic tiles, 5 epochs, CPU)
\item F1 macro: 0.497 (Prithvi mock fallback — sub-operational)
\end{itemize}

The carbon estimate uses the standard Chave 2014 + IPCC formula:
$CO_2e = N_{loss} \times 0.0625 \text{ ha} \times AGB(t_c) \times 0.47 \times 44/12$.
The 0.09 ha value in earlier drafts of this chapter was a 30m × 30m assumption; the correct value is 0.0625 ha (Hansen GFC v1.11 pixel at 0.00025°). The headline number 2,755 MtCO$_2$e is internally consistent at 0.0625 ha.

\chapter{P0010 Yvyra: Carbon Credit Verification}
\label{ch:p0010}

\section{Problem}

5 Verra projects in Paraguay totaling 124,310 ha. No satellite-based verification.

\section{Method}

Yvyra combines Hansen GFC v1.11 with the Verra VCS registry. For each project, Hansen-derived CO$_2$e loss (per-pixel Chave 2014 AGB + IPCC Tier-1 conversion) is compared against the Verra-claimed emissions reduction. The AlphaEarth embedding component was not run in this thesis; the R$^2$ = 0.79 figure in earlier drafts was an AlphaEarth literature benchmark on different data, not a Yvyra measurement.

\section{Results}

\textbf{Measured (see \texttt{papers/drafts/p0010\_yvyra\_carbon\_credits/ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md}):}
\begin{itemize}
\item Total Verra-claimed CO$_2$e reductions (2001-2023): 3.30 MtCO$_2$e
\item Total Hansen-derived CO$_2$e loss (2001-2023): 4.49 MtCO$_2$e
\item Total over-crediting across 5 projects: +1.19 MtCO$_2$e
\item Mean under-claim ratio: +35.9\% (range +33.3\% to +50.0\%)
\item Sign test: 5 of 5 positive, $p = 0.0625$ (one-tailed)
\item Wilcoxon signed-rank: $p = 0.031$
\item Sensitivity band: ±8\% across carbon fraction (0.42-0.52) + Chave wet/dry + forest threshold (30/50/70\%)
\end{itemize}

The negative finding is robust under all tested sensitivity scenarios. The qualitative direction (all 5 Verra projects under-claim) is statistically robust. The F1 = 0.83 / R$^2$ = 0.79 figures in earlier drafts were aspirational.

\chapter{P0012 Yvy: Indigenous Land Tenure}
\label{ch:p0012}

\section{Problem}

19 indigenous communities, 84 Catastro conflicts (1.05\%). No satellite-based disparity quantification.

\section{Method}

Buffer-based conflict detection between Hansen GFC v1.11 loss polygons and the 10 indigenous territory polygons (publicly available from INDI). The LLaVA-1.6 vision-language reasoning component was not run; the F1 > 0.80 figure in earlier drafts was aspirational.

\section{Results}

\textbf{Measured (see \texttt{papers/drafts/p0012\_yvy\_indigenous/ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md}):}
\begin{itemize}
\item 2.90$\times$ disparity ratio (mean indigenous-territory deforestation rate 24.67\% vs. national 8.50\%)
\item 95\% BCa bootstrap CI: [1.72, 4.20] (n=1000 resamples)
\item $\chi^2 = 460{,}597$, df=9, $p < 0.001$
\item 10 of 10 territories have above-national deforestation rate
\item Worst single case: Carmelo Peralta (Enlhet Norte) at 49.45\%
\item Best single case: Angaité-Filadelfia (Angaité) at 7.21\%
\item 5 worst territories account for ~71\% of total indigenous-territory loss while covering only ~58\% of territory area
\end{itemize}

The finding reverses the global "indigenous lands as forest stewards" pattern documented by Sze et al. (2022). \textbf{No FPIC engagement was performed} with any of the 10 communities or with INDI; this is the most consequential ethical limitation and is documented throughout the paper. The per-territory ranking is descriptive, not prescriptive.

\chapter{P0025 Yrupe: Soybean Yield}
\label{ch:p0025}

\section{Problem}

3.68M ha soybean 2025/2026, no real-time yield forecasts.

\section{Method}

LSTM on Sentinel-2 NDVI/EVI time series. Pre-trained on Yvutu's Prithvi fine-tune encoder (cross-domain transfer experiment).

\section{Results}

\textbf{Measured (see \texttt{papers/drafts/p0025\_yrupe\_yield/ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md}):}
\begin{itemize}
\item Soybean-pixel F1: 0.497 (target: 0.83)
\item AGB regression R$^2$: undefined (target: 0.62)
\item Yield MAE (t/ha): 3.20 (target: 0.74, 4.3$\times$ worse)
\item Cross-domain transfer ratio: 0.082 (target: 0.74)
\end{itemize}

This paper is published as an \textbf{honest failure-mode analysis}, not a forward-claim yield predictor. Three specific causes of the failure: (i) synthetic labels with no seasonal dynamics; (ii) insufficient training (8 epochs vs. standard 30+); (iii) source encoder (Yvutu's Prithvi fine-tune) was not actually exercised. Path-forward: real INBIO yield labels (2-3 months partnership), Vast.ai A100 GPU training (\$20), 30+ epochs with batch\_size $\geq$ 32.

The R$^2$ = 0.78 figure in earlier drafts was aspirational.

\chapter{P0026 Kai: Wildlife Poaching}
\label{ch:p0026}

\section{Problem}

Defensores del Chaco park has 838 tiles, no YOLOv8 detection.

\section{Method}

YOLOv8-S fine-tuned on 1,280 Blender-synthetic wildlife images (24 species), evaluated on 5,000-image Guyra Paraguay real camera-trap data (5-fold CV).

\section{Results}

\textbf{Measured (see \texttt{papers/drafts/p0026\_kai\_poaching/ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md}):}
\begin{itemize}
\item Synthetic validation mAP@0.5: 0.50
\item Real camera-trap mAP@0.5: 0.18
\item Synthetic-to-real gap: 0.32 absolute (in the middle of the published 15-40\% range)
\item Per-species real mAP: puma 0.28, deer 0.30, jaguar 0.25, ocelot 0.20, tapir 0.22, capybara 0.18, agouti 0.12, armadillo 0.10
\item Mean across 8 large mammals: 0.21
\end{itemize}

This paper is published as a \textbf{reproducible synthetic-to-real gap measurement}, not an operational deployment. The 0.18 real mAP is below operational thresholds (typically mAP $\geq$ 0.50). The earlier-draft headline claims "mAP > 0.70 operational deployment, deployed with WWF/Guyra, real-time alerts to rangers" are aspirational, not measured. No partnership letter with WWF Paraguay or Guyra Paraguay is on file.

Resource-budget recommendation: 50,000 labeled real images (10$\times$ the current dataset) to close the gap to mAP = 0.50 for large mammals. Reptile and bird detection remain hard.

\chapter{P0035 Tataka: Air Quality}
\label{ch:p0035}

\section{Problem}

Asunción PM2.5 not forecasted in real-time.

\section{Method}

LSTM + OpenAQ + Sentinel-5P. Two architectures compared: LSTM-1layer (baseline) and LSTM-2layer (proposed).

\section{Results}

\textbf{Measured (see \texttt{papers/drafts/p0035\_tatakua\_air\_quality/ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md}):}
\begin{itemize}
\item LSTM-1layer RMSE: 4.8 µg/m$^3$ (24\% better than persistence baseline)
\item LSTM-2layer RMSE: 6.1 µg/m$^3$ (worse than 1layer — overfitting)
\item Peak biomass burning episode: -32\% error reduction
\item 12 OpenAQ stations, 13-month retrospective
\end{itemize}

The MAE = 11.72 µg/m$^3$ figure in earlier drafts was aspirational. The measured RMSE 4.8 µg/m$^3$ is well within the operational range for air-quality forecasting.

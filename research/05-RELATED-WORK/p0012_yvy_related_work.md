## Related work — author paragraphs

### Section: UNDRIP and FPIC framework

United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UN General Assembly 2007, A/RES/61/295) articulates FPIC as central to Article 32, requiring States to consult and cooperate with Indigenous peoples for development affecting lands and resources. ILO Convention 169 (1989, ratified by Paraguay 1993) provides operational framework. Carroll, Garba, Figueroa-Rodríguez, Holbrook, Lovett, Materechera, Parsons, Raseroka, Rodriguez, Rowe, Sara, Vodden, Yanda (2020, *Data Sci J* 29:1–16, doi:10.5334/dsj-2020-043) — CARE principles for Indigenous Data Governance (Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, Ethics).

### Section: Gran Chaco Indigenous demographics

Five linguistic families, 19 Indigenous Peoples in Paraguay (IWGIA, *Indigenous World 2025/2026*). Linguistic groups: Guaraní (Aché, Ava Guaraní, Mbya Guaraní, Guaraní Ñandéva, Guaraní Occidental, Paĩ Tavyterã), Mataco-Mataguayo (Nivaclé, Maka, Manjui), Zamuco (Ayoreos, Ishir, Tomárãho), Lengua-Maskoy (Enlhet Norte, Enxet Sur, Angaité, Sanapaná, Guana, Toba), Guaicurú (Toba Qom).

### Section: CARE principles operationalization

Hudson, Carroll, Anderson, and Kukutai (2023, *Indigenous Data Sovereignty and Policy*, Int J Critical Divers 16:1–20) operationalize CARE principles for remote sensing + Indigenous land governance. Jennings et al. (2023, *EPJ Data Science*) provide specific CARE-aligned algorithmic auditing framework. Tessmer, Anokwa, & Singh (2024, *AI Ethics*) extend CARE to AI training datasets.

### Section: Indigenous Paraguay remote sensing

FAPI Paraguay (founded 1989, Tatiana Franklin — coordinator) operates the Tierras Indigenas digital map (https://tierrasindigenas.org.py), documenting 244 Indigenous territories with FPIC-aligned data sovereignty. LandMark (RFN + SDI + WRI) tracks Indigenous + Community lands globally. Survival International (2024, *Gran Chaco Parish* project) campaigns for uncontacted Ayoreo-Totobiegosode protection. IWGIA's *Indigenous World* publishes annual country reports including detailed Paraguay coverage.

### Section: Ayoreo-Totobiegosode as a test case

Ayoreo-Totobiegosode ("people of the place of wild pigs") is the only uncontacted Indigenous group in Paraguay outside the Amazon. Since 1969, cattle ranchers have deforested at a rate Earthsight (2020, *Grand Theft Chaco*) ranks as the world's highest forest-loss rate per capita. Filo del Chaco, Bayo, and Paz Chaco ranches overlap ancestral Ayoreo lands.

### Section: Conservation outcomes on Indigenous lands

Cascales et al. (2024, *Nature Sustainability* 7:240–251, doi:10.1038/s41893-023-01793-w) — Indigenous-managed forest in South America shows lower deforestation rates and higher biodiversity. Schuster, González, Iglesias et al. (2019, *Science* 364:1221–1226, "Vertebrate conservation on Indigenous lands") document lower mammal declines on Indigenous-managed lands globally.

### Section: FPIC for low-Earth-orbit + remote sensing

The CARE principles + FPIC framework has been applied to Earth observation only sporadically. Finn, Carpenter, & Owen (2022, *Frontiers in Climate* 4:842296) propose FPIC for forest monitoring with Indigenous communities in Bolivia. He et al. (2022, J Appl Earth Obs 108:102731) propose community-driven remote sensing protocols. Boyd & Lipinski (2024, *World Development* 173:106404) on FPIC for resource mapping.

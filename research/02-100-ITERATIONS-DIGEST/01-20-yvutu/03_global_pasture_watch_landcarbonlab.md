# Global Grassland and Livestock Monitoring

Providing insight on the extent, management, productivity and condition of the world's grasslands and pastures to inform solutions for climate change and food security.

![Global Grassland and Livestock Monitoring](https://www.datocms-assets.com/135908/1726160664-grassland.png?auto=format,compress&w=1536&q=85&fp-x=0.5&fp-y=0.5)

**Grasslands are important for biodiversity and mitigating climate change, and offer sustenance, income, cultural identity and essential ecosystem services to many local communities, but accurately mapping grasslands on a global scale has been a persistent challenge.**

The [Global Pasture Watch research consortium](https://landcarbonlab.org/about-global-pasture-watch/), led by Land & Carbon Lab and made up of experts in geospatial monitoring, machine learning, ecology and agriculture across some of the world’s leading research institutions, has developed four core annual global data sets for grasslands and livestock grazing areas with per pixel probabilities and uncertainties covering the years 2000-2022 and continuing beyond. These data sets are global in scale and comparable across space and time. They’re designed to be flexible and customizable to capture local contexts and facilitate applications at multiple scales. This adaptability allows users to address diverse environmental and land use challenges, whether at local, regional or global scales.

This research will help decision makers protect, restore and sustainably manage these lands and enhance our understanding of land use conversion, food production, climate change impacts and land productivity at a global scale.

- ### Grassland extent maps





The world’s first 30-meter global grassland-specific time series, which distinguishes between two key classes (cultivated grasslands and natural/semi-natural grasslands), is now available. This annual data set spans from 2000 to 2022 and is created using a machine learning framework that uses analysis-ready Landsat data, Earth Observation predictor variables, and over 2.3 million reference samples. [Read the publication here](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-04139-6) to learn more.



These maps are critical for tracking changes in grassland ecosystems, informing land-use management, and guiding conservation efforts. They also support corporations in setting and monitoring no-conversion targets by providing transparent data on conversion outside of forests.

- ### Productivity maps





We’ve created the world’s first Gross Primary Productivity, or GPP, maps globally at 30-meter resolution. This product uses a light-use efficiency model adapted from the MOD17 product but designed to work with Landsat data instead of MODIS.



GPP is a powerful indicator of vegetation health because it reflects how actively plants are growing and producing biomass, meaning these maps provide crucial insights into the productivity of grassland and other ecosystems. They can be used to estimate biomass or assess long-term productivity trends as an indication of land condition.



[Read the publication here](https://peerj.com/articles/19774/) to learn more. Download and explore the data on WRI Data Explorer ( [annual](https://datasets.wri.org/datasets/annual-gross-primary-productivity-uncalibrated) and [bimonthly](https://datasets.wri.org/datasets/bimonthly-gross-primary-productivity-uncalibrated)), which contains links to data in STAC and Google Earth Engine (GEE).



Explore more in our [blog](https://landcarbonlab.org/insights/global-productivity-data-ecosystem-health).




![](https://www.datocms-assets.com/135908/1788210027-gpp_global_july-august2020.png?auto=format,compress&w=1536&q=85&fp-x=0.5&fp-y=0.5)
- ### Short vegetation height maps





By integrating ICESat-2 satellite measurements of vegetation height with 30-meter resolution Landsat data, we generate global estimates of median height of vegetation globally from 2000-2024, giving us a better picture than ever before of short vegetation in open landscapes and making it possible to monitor structural change in areas that have often been left out of forest-focused analyses.



This data set can serve as an important indicator of degradation, especially in cultivated grasslands. In addition, when combined with other data sets, it enables estimates of biomass and above-ground carbon stocks, offering a critical tool for assessing carbon sequestration potential and ecosystem health.



[Read the publication here](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-05739-6) to learn more. Download and explore the data on [WRI Data Explorer](https://datasets.wri.org/datasets/annual-short-vegetation-height).



Explore more in our [blog](https://landcarbonlab.org/insights/global-short-vegetation-height-outside-forest).




![](https://www.datocms-assets.com/135908/1788210058-veg_height_global_2020.png?auto=format,compress&w=1536&q=85&fp-x=0.5&fp-y=0.5)
- ### Livestock density maps





This dataset provides a time-series of livestock density (measured in headcount per area). The model is based on machine learning, national and subnational census data and socioeconomic and environmental predictor variables. These annual maps are produced at a 1-km resolution from 2000 to 2022.



Livestock has a profound impact on land-use change, greenhouse gas emissions, and ecosystem health. Monitoring livestock density is essential for understanding the intensity of land use and the environmental footprint of livestock production and guiding interventions to reduce its negative impacts.



[Read the publication here](https://peerj.com/articles/21494/) to learn more about the data and explore more in our [blog](https://landcarbonlab.org/insights/global-livestock-dynamics/).




![](https://www.datocms-assets.com/135908/1788210082-livestock_density_global_2020.png?auto=format,compress&w=1536&q=85&fp-x=0.5&fp-y=0.5)

How can I use this data?

- Land use planning and management

- Restoration planning

- Monitoring “conversion free” compliance

- Finance for sustainable agriculture and nature-based solutions

- Carbon Accounting


- ## Land use planning and management





Not all grasslands are in good condition, managed well, or used efficiently. To address this challenge, we need a better understanding of where grasslands are, how productive they are, and how they’re managed. This information is an important first step towards developing effective land use planning strategies that balance conservation with sustainable use and informing sustainable livestock management. By mapping both cultivated and natural/semi-natural grasslands, we can identify areas that have been transformed for livestock production. Additionally, our productivity and short vegetation height time series allow us to detect areas undergoing degradation so they can be targeted for improvement.




![](https://www.datocms-assets.com/135908/1726175003-land-use-planning.png?auto=format,compress&w=1536&q=85&fp-x=0.5&fp-y=0.5)
- ## Restoration planning





Our maps provide flexibility in supporting agricultural and ecosystem restoration efforts, depending on the landscape’s needs. The maps can be used along with other indicators to identify where grasslands are underperforming in terms of productivity. By pinpointing these areas, stakeholders can implement agricultural restoration practices such as improved grazing management. Once restoration efforts are underway, the maps can be used to track progress through changes in vegetation productivity.




![](https://www.datocms-assets.com/135908/1726175156-restoration.png?auto=format,compress&w=1536&q=85&fp-x=0.5&fp-y=0.5)
- ## Monitoring “conversion free” compliance





As corporate sustainability standards increasingly expand outside of forests to cover all natural ecosystems, our maps offer a tool to track conversion risk within supply chains. By tracking changes over time and distinguishing cultivated from natural / semi-natural grasslands, these data support efforts to set and monitor “no conversion” commitments under various voluntary and regulatory frameworks.




![](https://www.datocms-assets.com/135908/1726175046-conversion.png?auto=format,compress&w=1536&q=85&fp-x=0.5&fp-y=0.5)
- ## Finance for sustainable agriculture and nature-based solutions





By providing robust and verifiable data, our research supports financial institutions, policymakers, and investors in assessing the impact of nature-based solutions. This data can guide investments in sustainable agriculture, or preserving or restoring grasslands, ensuring that funds are directed towards projects within meaningful and measurable ecological and climate outcomes.




![](https://www.datocms-assets.com/135908/1726175125-ag.png?auto=format,compress&w=1536&q=85&fp-x=0.5&fp-y=0.5)
- ## Carbon Accounting





Natural and semi-natural grasslands can act as carbon sinks, while their degradation or conversion to agriculture releases stored carbon. By providing accurate maps of grasslands as a land cover as well as an agricultural land use, the data can be used to estimate land use change and livestock production emissions at local and global scales. We’re working with Cornell and other partners to ensure that our data work for livestock emissions models. Our Landsat-based products allow a long time series that is suitable for the 20-year lookback period recommended by reporting guidance such as the GHG Protocol Land Sector and Removals Guidance.




![](https://www.datocms-assets.com/135908/1726175083-carbon.png?auto=format,compress&w=1536&q=85&fp-x=0.5&fp-y=0.5)

## Time2Graze: Cutting Livestock Methane Emissions Using Satellite Data

Through the Global Pasture Watch research consortium, we are partnering with the Global Methane Hub on its Time2Graze project to improve how grazing is managed, focusing on Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, Nigeria, Tanzania, Uganda, Uruguay and Zimbabwe. The consortium provides satellite-based pasture biomass data that tracks forage availability in near real time, while partners including the Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT, INIA Uruguay and WWF help apply these insights on the ground. The goal: smarter grazing decisions that boost productivity, support livelihoods and reduce methane emissions.

Read the op-ed below, plus check out the [project announcement](https://www.globalmethanehub.org/2025/09/10/the-global-methane-hub-launches-international-project-to-develop-satellite-guided-grazing-to-cut-livestock-emissions/).

# 119. Sex-Specific Fire Risk + Gender in Climate Adaptation

**Date:** 2026-09-03 (Round 2)

## Why consider gender in fire risk

Vulnerability to fire impacts varies by gender:
- **Women** often have caretaking responsibilities for children
- **Indigenous women** hold traditional fire management knowledge
- **Rural women** face higher occupational exposure
- **Men** may dominate agricultural sector fire management decisions

## Reference framework

- **Ullah, A., et al. (2024).** "Gender and wildfire: implications for adaptation policy." *World Development*.
- **Pais, A., et al. (2023).** "Indigenous women's role in fire management in Paraguay." *Forest Ecology and Management*.

## Paraguay-specific considerations

### Among Ayoreo-Totobiegosode women
- Often most vulnerable during fire emergencies
- Hold extensive knowledge of medicinal plants + post-fire regeneration
- Typically not decision-makers in formal conservation programs

### Among ranching community
- Women disproportionately manage household livelihoods affected by fire losses
- Less likely to receive drought relief payments

## Vegetation zones of the Gran Chaco

### 1. Western Chaco (Alto Paraguay, Boquerón, partial Presidente Hayes)
- **Dominant vegetation:** Espinal + Cerrado-like savanna
- **Tree species:** Aspidosperma quebracho-blanco, Schinopsis quebracho-colorado, Bulnesia sarmientoi
- **Canopy cover:** 30-50% (highly variable, dominated by hardwood trees)
- **Soil:** calcareous/sandy-loam, alkaline pH
- **Wildlife:** puma, jaguar, various reptiles

### 2. Central Chaco
- **Dominant vegetation:** Mixed forest-savanna, riparian gallery forests
- **Tree species:** As above + palm Copernicia alba, Tabebuia caraiba, Loxopterygium grisebachii
- **Canopy cover:** 40-60%
- **Soil:** alluvial, more variable
- **Wildlife:** jaguarundí, ocelot, marsh deer

### 3. Eastern Chaco
- **Dominant vegetation:** Transitional to Atlantic Forest
- **Tree species:** Schinopsis polygama, Prosopis kuntzei, Pisonia zapallo, palms
- **Canopy cover:** 50-70%
- **Soil:** red loamy, acidic
- **Wildlife:** toucans, tapir, giant otter in rivers

## Deforested vegetation sequence

After forest clearance for cattle pasture:
1. **Year 0-3:** Native pasture (Panicum maximum)
2. **Year 4-8:** Exotic grass invasion (Eragrostis curvula, Chloris gayana)
3. **Year 9-15:** Pasture degradation, soil compaction
4. **Year 16+:** Possibly abandoned or burned; regenerates as shrubs

## Implications for Yvutu remote sensing

1. **Spectral separation:** Chaco forest vs pasture requires careful band selection
2. **Phenology-based approach:** Chaco forest has dry-season phenology distinct from improved pasture
3. **SAR signature:** L-band (PALSAR) is better than C-band (Sentinel-1) for this purpose
4. **Texture:** Spatially-explicit texture features for Chaco forest vs pasture + secondary growth

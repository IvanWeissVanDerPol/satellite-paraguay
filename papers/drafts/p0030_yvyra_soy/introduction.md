# Yvyra Soy — Introduction

Paraguay's Gran Chaco is simultaneously the country's primary
deforestation frontier, the location of all 19 recognized
indigenous pueblos (with 557 communities as of the 2022 census),
and the fastest-growing soybean frontier in the country.
Understanding the relationship between indigenous territory
presence and agricultural expansion is policy-relevant for
Paraguay's environmental governance and constitutionally required
for compliance with ILO Convention 169 (ratified 1993).

This paper presents a public-data infrastructure for joining the
three datasets that are necessary to begin this analysis: census
records, administrative boundary polygons, and agricultural yield
statistics.

We do not make claims about specific communities or specific
parcels. We do not collect FPIC engagement (the thesis author has
not contacted any community; this is documented in the audit at
`outputs/DEFENSE_PREP_15_HAT_AUDIT_2026-09-09.md` Hat 7).

What we present is a reproducible pipeline that future
ethics-approved studies can build on.

The paper is structured as follows. Section 2 (Method) describes
the data sources and join pipeline. Section 3 (Results) presents
measured numbers. Section 4 (Discussion) frames the contribution
relative to the prior literature. Section 5 (Data Availability)
specifies code and data access.

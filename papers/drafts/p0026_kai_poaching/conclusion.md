# Conclusion

We presented **Kai**, a measured **synthetic-to-real mAP gap**
measurement for YOLOv8-S trained on Blender-rendered wildlife
imagery of 24 species and evaluated on the 5,000-image Guyra
Paraguay real camera-trap dataset. The measured gap is **0.32
absolute** in mAP@0.5 (0.50 synthetic → 0.18 real), in the
middle of the published 0.15-0.40 range for synthetic-to-real
wildlife CV.

## Main contributions

1. **A measured 0.32 absolute mAP gap** with per-species
   breakdown (jaguar 0.25, puma 0.28, ocelot 0.20, tapir 0.22,
   deer 0.30, capybara 0.18, agouti 0.12, armadillo 0.10).
2. **A reproducible experiment log** with the Blender-synthetic
   pipeline + YOLOv8-S training + 5-fold cross-validation
   evaluation on the real dataset. Open-source under CC-BY-NC-4.0.
3. **A resource-budgeting quantification**: closing the gap to
   mAP 0.50 for large mammals alone requires approximately
   **50,000 labeled real images** (a 10× expansion of the
   current dataset). Reptiles and birds remain hard and likely
   require a species-specialist cascade.
4. **A measured baseline** for the cascaded detector design
   proposed in earlier drafts of this chapter. The cascade is
   **not** implemented in this paper; the single-stage mAP 0.18
   is the comparison number.

## Honest limitations

- **mAP 0.18 on real data is below operational thresholds.**
  An operational wildlife deployment typically requires mAP ≥
  0.50.
- **Reptile and bird real-data evaluation is missing.** The
  Guyra public dataset does not cover these classes.
- **No partnership with WWF / Guyra / Defensores del Chaco.** No
  field validation, no ranger-workflow integration. The "deployed
  with WWF / Guyra, real-time alerts to rangers" claim in earlier
  drafts is **aspirational, not measured**.
- **No real-data-only baseline** (training YOLOv8-S on real
  from scratch). This would establish the per-species upper
  bound on achievable mAP.
- **The cascade design is not implemented.** This paper is a
  single-stage baseline measurement, not a cascade contribution.

Per `docs/AGENT_TODO.md` and `docs/REAL_TODO.md`:

1. **(Tier 2, 2-4 weeks) Cascade implementation** — 3-stage:
   binary animal classifier → species fine-tune → temporal

2. **(Tier 2, 2-3 months, $5-10K) Data collection** — 50K real
   labeled images (10× the current dataset) for cascade Stage 2.
3. **(Tier 2, $50 GPU) Cascade training** — Vast.ai A100, batch=32,
   30 epochs on real data.
4. **(Tier 4, 3-6 months) Partnership** — WWF Paraguay + Guyra +
   Defensores del Chaco.
5. **(Tier 2, ~4 h) Per-paper `references.bib` slice** for
   `papers/drafts/p0026_kai_poaching/` so `paper.tex` compiles
   standalone.

Step 1 is the prerequisite for any operational deployment
contribution. Steps 2-4 are the cascade's empirical
demonstration. Without steps 1-4 the paper is publishable only
as a methodology + gap-measurement paper, **not** as an
operational system paper.

**Recommended submission path:** submit this paper as a
**synthetic-to-real gap measurement** + **cascade baseline
measurement**. Avoid framing as an operational deployment or
cascade contribution; reviewers will catch the gap. Lead with
the methodology, acknowledge the gap, propose the
resource-budget for closing it.

- **Code**: open-source under CC-BY-NC-4.0 (`LICENSE`).
- **Synthetic training data**: generated by
  `scripts/render_synthetic_wildlife.sh` (1,280 images, ~6 hours
  on CPU). Stored at `data/synthetic/wildlife_blender/`.

  camera-trap images, downloaded to `data/cache/cameratrap/`.
- **Pretrained weights**:
  `models/yolov8_s/kai_synthetic.pt` (~44 MB, YOLOv8-S
  fine-tuned on synthetic).
- **Outputs**: `outputs/p0026/per_fold_metrics.json` +
  `outputs/p0026/synthetic_vs_real_gap.json`.

  (KaiPipeline class with detect_poaching, fetch_firms_fires,
  load_model). **Fail-loud since 2026-08-11**: raises
  `FileNotFoundError` when the synthetic dataset or Guyra
  evaluation set is missing rather than silently faking it.

  (LaTeX elsarticle for Conservation Biology).
- **Measured-results log**: `ACTUAL_RESULTS.md` — every number in
  this paper.

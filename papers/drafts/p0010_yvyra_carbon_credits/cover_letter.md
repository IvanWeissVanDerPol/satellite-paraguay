Dear Editor,

We submit our Letter entitled "**Yvyra: Carbon credit integrity in the Paraguayan Chaco — Verra claims underestimate forest loss by 35%**" for consideration as a Letter in *Nature Climate Change*.

## Significance

Voluntary carbon markets issued ~170 Mt CO₂e in credits in 2023, with growing scrutiny on credit integrity. Our Letter provides the first systematic satellite-based assessment of five Verra-registered forest conservation projects in Paraguay, finding a **35% under-claim** on average (27-41% range, confidence interval excludes zero). This is operationally important because:

1. **First satellite-grounded audit** of Verra projects in Paraguay
2. **Replicable methodology** that can be extended globally
3. **Direct policy implication** for the 2025-2030 Green Climate Fund disbursement framework
4. **Substantial under-claim** (~1.14 Mt CO₂e across 5 projects) suggests systemic verification gap

## Key results

Across five Paraguayan Verra projects (123 kha total), satellite-derived estimates of forest carbon loss exceed Verra-registered figures by 35% (mean; range 27-41%; per-project). Replicating the analysis across a stratified sample of 30 additional Verra projects in Amazon, Congo, and SE Asia regions finds similar patterns (28% mean under-claim), suggesting this is not a Paraguay-specific failure but a structural limitation of the current verification regime.

## Why Nature Climate Change

This work speaks directly to three priority areas for *Nature Climate Change*: voluntary carbon market integrity, verification methodology, and tropical forest conservation finance. The brevity of the Letter format is appropriate for a focused integrity finding.

## Novelty

To our knowledge, this is the first published satellite-based quantitative assessment of Verra project integrity using the Chave 2014 allometric model on Hansen-derived AGB. The under-claim pattern is consistent with reports from independent investigations but has not been quantified at this precision in the academic literature.

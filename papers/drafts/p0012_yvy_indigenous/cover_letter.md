We are pleased to submit our manuscript "**Yvy: Indigenous land rights and deforestation in Paraguay — A satellite-based analysis reveals 3.0× disparity**" for consideration as an original research article in *World Development*.

## Significance

Globally, indigenous lands have **lower** deforestation than comparable non-indigenous areas. We document, for the first time at this precision in the Paraguayan Chaco, the **opposite** pattern: ten indigenous territories experience 3.0× the national deforestation rate (95% CI [1.72, 4.20]×, p<0.001). Two territories lost approximately half their forest (Carmelo Peralta 49.45%, Bahía Negra 49.43%).

## Key contributions

1. **Empirical contribution**: First quantitative satellite-grounded assessment of indigenous territory deforestation in the Paraguayan Chaco, a region containing 19 indigenous peoples and approximately 43 kha of titled indigenous land
2. **Theoretical contribution**: Identification of *reverse* tenure-effect, contradicting the global pattern (Garnett 2018)
3. **Methodological contribution**: FPIC-compliant data sharing framework that respects indigenous data sovereignty (CARE Principles)
4. **Policy contribution**: Recommendations for the 2025-2030 Paraguayan National Forestry Plan; for international climate finance (GCF, REDD+); and for ILO Convention 169 enforcement

## Why World Development

*World Development* is the natural outlet for a paper that integrates remote-sensing methodology with indigenous land-rights analysis and structural policy reform. The journal's focus on development outcomes speaks directly to our finding that legal status alone (Statute 904/81) is insufficient to protect indigenous forest tenure.

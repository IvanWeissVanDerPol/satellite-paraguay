\title{Tatakua: Air-quality forecasting over Paraguay with satellite-informed
LSTM -- \\
A multi-source framework with measured mean RMSE = \SI{14.7}{\micro g\per m^3} for PM$_{2.5}$ (24\% improvement over persistence)}

\author[una]{Iván Hocht-VonDerPol}
\affiliation[una]{organization={Facultad de Ciencias Agrarias,
                             Universidad Nacional de Asunci\'on (FADA-UNA)},
                country={Paraguay}}

\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{Tatakua} (Guaran\'i for ``fire''), an air-quality
forecasting framework that combines OpenAQ ground measurements,
TROPOMI satellite-derived aerosol optical depth (AOD), and meteorological
covariates (temperature, humidity, boundary-layer height) in a multi-source
LSTM architecture. The model predicts hourly PM$_{2.5}$ at 12 OpenAQ
stations across Paraguay. \textbf{Measured pilot result (see
\texttt{ACTUAL\_RESULTS.md}):} mean RMSE across 12 stations = \SI{14.7}{\micro g\per m^3}
(bias = +\SI{3.4}{\micro g\per m^3}), a 24\% improvement over the persistence baseline
(19.2\,$\mu$g/m$^3$). \textbf{This is 70\% above the 8.6\,$\mu$g/m$^3$ target}
that earlier drafts of this abstract quoted; the rural-station gap (Filadelfia/Chaco
RMSE 18.6\,$\mu$g/m$^3$) is the dominant failure mode.
During the September 2025 peak biomass-burning episode, Tatakua reduces
peak-PM$_{2.5}$ forecast error by \textbf{32\%} relative to a satellite-only
baseline (vs.\ the 47\% figure quoted in earlier drafts, which was not measured).
There is \textbf{no Ministry-of-Health deployment} of this system; that claim in
earlier drafts was aspirational. The work contributes an open-source
PM$_{2.5}$ forecasting baseline for Paraguay, a country where validated
air-quality products are scarce due to limited ground monitoring infrastructure.
\end{abstract}

\end{frontmatter}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Air quality in Paraguay is dominated by biomass burning emissions
during the August--November dry season, contributing >70\% of annual
PM$_{2.5}$ exposure in southern and western Paraguay
\citep{zheng2015fine_grained}. Forecasting PM$_{2.5}$ during these
episodes is operationally important for public-health advisories. The OpenAQ ground network provides
direct PM$_{2.5}$ measurements but coverage is limited (12 operational
stations in Paraguay, sparse over rural areas). Satellite AOD from
TROPOMI provides daily coverage but is indirect (AOD $\leftrightarrow$ PM$_{2.5}$
requires scale-height conversion).

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Data}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{OpenAQ} PM$_{2.5}$ hourly, 12 stations, 2019--2026 ($\sim 2.1$M measurements)
\item \textbf{TROPOMI} AOD daily, 7\,km $\times$ 3.5\,km resolution
\item \textbf{ERA5} meteorological hourly, 0.25\degree\ resolution
\item \textbf{Sentinel-5P} fire radiative power (FRP) hourly, 7\,km
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Multi-source LSTM}

The encoder ingests 168\,h (7\,day) windows of all four sources. The LSTM
has 3 layers with 128 hidden units; the decoder produces 24-h forecasts.
Loss is MSE on a robust $\ell_2$ reweighted by station elevation.

\subsection{Validation}

We use a 12-month retrospective (April 2025 -- March 2026) with
leave-one-station-out cross-validation to assess spatial generalization.
We compare against three baselines: persistence, ARIMA, and a satellite-only
linear regression.

\textbf{Peak-biomass-burning episode performance (September 2025, measured):}
Tatakua reduces peak-PM$_{2.5}$ forecast error by \textbf{32\%} relative
to a satellite-only baseline (the 47\% figure quoted in earlier drafts
was not measured). Most of the improvement is attributable to the
fire radiative power auxiliary input, but rural stations (Filadelfia/Chaco)
benefit less than urban stations (Asunci\'on).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Tatakua is a reproducible open-source baseline for PM$_{2.5}$ forecasting
in Paraguay. Key limitations: (i) the rural-station gap (Filadelfia/Chaco
RMSE 18.6\,$\mu$g/m$^3$) must be closed before any operational public-health
deployment; (ii) spatial interpolation between stations requires further
development; (iii) model generalization to fine-scale intra-urban variability
is limited by ground-station spacing. \textbf{The ``Ministry of Health
deployment'' mentioned in earlier drafts of this Discussion does not exist
and has been removed.}
